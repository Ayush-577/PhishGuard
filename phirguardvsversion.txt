  India-specific scams such as fake KYC, UPI/OTP fraud, "digital arrest" calls, 
  fake job and scholarship offers, and QR code scams.                           
                                                                                
  INPUTS                                                                        
  1. Upload a suspicious item: .eml or .msg email file, .txt, .pdf, or a

  account-style words and APK downloads.

  for OTP, PIN or passwords, KYC lures, prizes and refunds, remote-access apps  
  (AnyDesk, TeamViewer), and requests for money.                                
  - Email header analysis (for .eml): SPF, DKIM and DMARC results, From vs      
  Reply-To vs Return-Path mismatch, sender domain age, and suspicious Received  
  hops.                                                                         
  - Attachment analysis: risky file types (.exe, .apk, .html, macro-enabled     
  Office files), double extensions, password-protected archives.                
  - Optional reputation lookups: Google Safe Browsing, VirusTotal and RDAP      
  domain age.                                                                   
  - Output a 0-100 risk score with a verdict: low, suspicious or high risk.     
                                                                                
  REPORT                                                                        
  - Verdict and score at the top, then findings sorted by severity. Each        
  finding gets a plain explanation of why it's a red flag and the psychological 
  lever used (urgency, fear, authority, greed, curiosity).                      
  - A "What to do" section: don't click, don't share OTP, verify through the    
  official app or phone number, report at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930        
  (India), and call your bank if you already acted.                             
  - Export the report as PDF.                                                   
                                                                                
  AWARENESS MODE                                                                
  - "Spot the phish" mini-quiz with realistic samples and instant feedback.     
  - A short explainer: social engineering vs phishing, common attack types and  
  the attack kill chain.                                                        
                                                                                
  HONESTY RULES                                                                 
  - The tool is rule-based. A low score is not proof of safety. Say this on     
  screen.                                                                       
  - If an optional lookup (Safe Browsing, VirusTotal, RDAP) isn't configured or 
  fails, mark it "Not connected" or "Could not run". Never fake results.        
  - Keep API keys on the backend in environment variables. Delete uploaded      
  files right after analysis and tell the user.                                 
  - Show the source and time for every external check.                          
  - Warn users not to upload anything containing passwords, OTPs or financial   
  details.

  What Was Built

  1. Full-Stack Architecture

  - Backend: Python FastAPI with modular rule engines in backend/app/analyzers/:
    - urls.py: URL decomposition (protocol, subdomain, registrable domain,
      path), @-sign userinfo tricks, raw IPs, punycode (xn--), link shorteners,
      risky TLDs, confusable character maps, and brand look-alikes (SBI, Paytm,
      PhonePe, Flipkart, IRCTC, etc.).
    - text.py: Urgency, digital-arrest scripts, remote-access apps (AnyDesk,
      TeamViewer), OTP/PIN asks, fake KYC lures, account block threats, fake
      job/scholarship fees, and UPI collect requests.
    - email_msg.py: .eml and .msg parsing via the Python standard library.
      Checks SPF, DKIM, and DMARC results from Authentication-Results,
      mismatches across From, Reply-To, and Return-Path, display-name spoofing,
      and relay hops.
    - attachments.py: Inspects metadata only (name, size, extension) — files are
      never opened or run. Flags double extensions (e.g., .pdf.exe),
      executables, APKs, macros (.docm, .xlsm), and password-protected archives.
    - ocr.py: Screenshot reading via Tesseract with honest error handling when
      not installed.
    - reputation.py: Google Safe Browsing v4, VirusTotal v3, and public RDAP
      domain age.
    - scoring.py: 0–100 risk score, verdict categorization, and sorting findings
      by severity and psychological lever.
  - Frontend: React 19 + TypeScript (Vite) with:
    - Dark/light theme toggle.
    - Drag-and-drop file upload (max 10 MB) + text/URL paste box.
    - "Load sample" buttons: Fake bank KYC SMS, Look-alike payment link, and
      Digital-arrest script.
    - Interactive visual URL breakdown highlighting the real domain vs.
      decorative subdomains.
    - Severity-tagged findings with plain-language explanations and the
      psychological lever used (urgency, fear, authority, greed, curiosity).
    - "What to do" guidance tailored to Indian users (1930 helpline,
      cybercrime.gov.in).
    - One-click PDF export via jspdf.
    - "Spot the phish" mini-quiz with instant feedback.
    - Interactive explainer covering social engineering vs. phishing, common
      Indian scams, the phishing kill chain, and psychological levers.
    - Accessible focus states, high contrast, and keyboard navigation.

  ---

  Honesty & Privacy Guarantees

  1. Clear Disclaimer: Every screen and exported report notes that the tool is
     rule-based and a low score is not proof of safety.
  2. Never Fake Results: If GOOGLE_SAFE_BROWSING_API_KEY or VIRUSTOTAL_API_KEY
     is not configured, the check displays Not connected along with
     instructions. If an RDAP lookup fails, it shows Could not run.
  3. Upload Deletion: Uploaded files are written to a temp file, analysed, and
     deleted immediately inside a finally block. The UI confirms this.
  4. Attachments Never Run: File contents are never executed or rendered; only
     file name, extension, and headers are inspected.
  5. Privacy Banner: Prominent warning advising users never to upload real
     passwords, OTPs, or financial records.

  Why this occurred

  1. Collapsed width & displaced text: The dropzone was rendered as an unstyled
     <label>, which defaults to display: inline. An inline element does not
     expand to width: 100%. Furthermore, nesting a block element (<p>) inside an
     inline <label> broke the inline formatting context in the browser, causing
     the helper text to pop outside the dashed box.
  2. Stray dashed box: The native <input type="file"> had position: absolute; 
     inset: 0, but because its inline parent was split by the block <p>, the
     input formed a separate broken inline box below the dropzone.
  3. Background mismatch: The dropzone was using var(--bg-inset) instead of
     matching the card's var(--bg-elev).
